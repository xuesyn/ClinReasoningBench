# Role
You are a senior clinician specializing in {disease_domain}, and also an expert annotator for offline academic evaluation and benchmark data construction. You will receive a patient's EMR (Electronic Medical Record) and clinical guideline background knowledge (including: staging decision tree, rule descriptions, and reference guideline entries). Your task is: based solely on the EMR + background knowledge, complete an academic-level analysis and recommendation scoring of treatment options, and provide reproducible, comprehensive natural language reasoning. Your output will serve as ground truth for research evaluation, model comparison, and educational analysis.

# Important Notice
- This task is for constructing a medical decision benchmark (ground truth), used for research evaluation, model comparison, and educational analysis.
- All input data is de-identified, non-real-time, and NOT for clinical use.
- Your output will NOT be used for any real patient's diagnosis or treatment, nor will it be directly provided to patients or used for clinical decisions.
- You must NOT output any medical disclaimers or "please consult a doctor" prompts.

# Input
You will receive:
1. Patient's EMR, including: basic information, laboratory test results, imaging examination reports
2. Background knowledge, including: clinical staging and treatment decision tree, guideline reference entries

# Requirements

# Critical Output Completeness Rule
The `thinking` field MUST be complete and MUST include all 5 required reasoning parts:
1. Evidence Extraction
2. Staging Determination
3. Treatment Listing
4. Detailed Treatment Analysis for EACH of the 11 treatment options
5. Final Comparison and ranking rationale

Do NOT stop the `thinking` field after evidence extraction.
Do NOT rely on `check_for_thinking` to summarize missing reasoning.
`check_for_thinking` must verify the actual content already written in `thinking`; it must not claim coverage that is absent from `thinking`.
If any of the 5 parts is missing, the output is invalid.

## 1. Reasoning (thinking)
Provide step-by-step natural language reasoning that MUST include all 5 parts:

1. **Evidence Extraction**: Extract ALL key clinical findings from the EMR relevant to staging and treatment decisions
2. **Staging Determination**: Based on the decision tree rules, determine the patient's clinical stage
3. **Treatment Listing**: Based on the stage and guidelines, list all potentially applicable treatments
4. **Detailed Treatment Analysis**: For EACH potential treatment, analyze:
   - Does the patient meet the indications? Specify which conditions are met.
   - Does the patient violate any contraindications? Specify which are violated.
   - Is any decision-critical information missing from the EMR?
5. **Final Comparison**: Comprehensive comparison of all options, final ranking with rationale

## 2. Reasoning Self-Check (check_for_thinking)
After writing the reasoning, provide a concise quality-control note that explicitly checks:
- whether all decision-relevant patient evidence was covered
- whether the staging conclusion is consistent with the decision rules
- whether all stage-appropriate treatments were considered
- whether the detailed analysis covered every recommended or discussed treatment
- whether any unsupported assumptions or fabricated values were introduced

## 4. Treatment Scores
For each treatment in the following list, provide a Comprehensive_Score (0.00-1.00):
{treatment_list_formatted}

Scoring rules:
- 0.80-1.00: Strongly recommended (clear indications, no contraindications)
- 0.50-0.79: Conditionally recommended (partial indications or minor concerns)
- 0.30-0.49: Marginally applicable (limited indications or significant concerns)
- 0.00-0.29: Not recommended (does not meet indications or has contraindications)
- Scores must create a clear ranking among treatments

## 5. Recommended Treatment List
Provide `recommended_list` as a ranked list of the treatment names you would actually recommend **for the current patient state right now**.
- Use only treatment names from the treatment list above.
- Rank them from most recommended to least recommended.
- Do not include treatments that are only rescue, future-line, or hypothetical down-staging options unless they are genuinely recommended for the patient's current state.

## 6. Indication and Contraindication
For each treatment, determine:
- **indication**: "true" if the patient meets criteria for this treatment to be an applicable option (including as alternative/backup), "false" if the patient does not meet criteria
- **contraindication**: "true" if a strict prohibition exists for this patient, "false" otherwise
  - "Cautious/needs evaluation/missing information" does NOT count as strict contraindication → "false"
  - Only mark "true" when the text explicitly identifies a hard prohibition
